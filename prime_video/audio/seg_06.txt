"The division table had been computing the wrong thing — a distance. But primes are defined by remainder: a prime is a number nothing smaller divides evenly. So we rebuilt the table honestly — each cell shows n mod d, the remainder. Now the primes weren't hidden in the numbers — they were hidden in the remainders. Gold rows are primes: no zero anywhere. Red rows are composites — and the zeros literally show the factorization. Nine has a zero under three. Fifteen has a zero under three. The intuition was right; the formula was one word away."
